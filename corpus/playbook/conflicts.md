# Where sources disagree

Two good sources contradicting each other is usually not one being wrong — it
is that they sell different things, at different price points, through
different channels, to differently sophisticated buyers. Record the
disagreement and the condition that resolves it. Never average them.

---

## Email length — how short is short

- **`gong-cold-email-data`** says: **100 words or fewer**, with **3–4 sentences**
  generating the highest reply rates. Context: B2B SaaS, Gong's customer base,
  28M+ emails, SDR-driven outbound.
- **`lavender-benchmarks`** says: optimal length is **25–50 words**. Context:
  B2B, 231,818 emails across ~50,000 inboxes of sellers already using an email
  coaching tool.
- **Resolution rule:** These are a ceiling and a target, not a true
  contradiction — 25–50 words satisfies both. **Write to Lavender's 25–50 and
  treat Gong's 100 as the hard ceiling you never cross.** Where a message
  genuinely cannot land in 50 words (technical buyer needing a concrete use
  case, per Lavender's own technical-buyer article), spend up to 100 and cut
  everything else. Note that Josh Braun's 4T template is 4 sentences and lands
  in this range — the three sources are compatible in practice.

## First-touch length vs. follow-up length

- **`lavender-benchmarks`** says both: first touch **25–50 words**, AND
  follow-ups with **4+ sentences get 15x more meetings** than follow-ups with
  three or fewer. This is internal to one source, not a cross-source conflict.
- **Resolution rule:** Length rules are **stage-specific**. Do not carry the
  25–50 word constraint into follow-ups — the data says the opposite there.
  Short to earn attention, longer to earn the meeting.

## Questions — banned or the entire method?

- **`lavender-benchmarks`** says: questions in the **subject line** lower open
  rates by **56%**; punctuation (?) costs another **36%**.
- **`gong-cold-email-data`** agrees for subject lines: "Avoid numbers and
  questions."
- **`josh-braun`** says: the **body** should be built around a question — the
  poke-the-bear question and the 4T "Think" sentence are both questions, and
  the close is a question.
- **Resolution rule:** **Not a conflict — a location rule.** Questions are
  prohibited in the subject line and are the core mechanism in the body. This
  is the single easiest place to misapply this corpus, so it is written down
  explicitly. Subject line: flat, boring, title-case, 1–3 words. Body:
  question-led.

## Personalization depth vs. volume — the real conflict

- **`lavender-benchmarks`** says: the 5x5x5 process — 5 minutes research, 5
  minutes writing, per email — and personalizing vs. templating is worth
  **+50% to +250%** reply rate. Context: B2B considered purchase, SDR or AE
  with a defined territory, high ACV.
- **`hormozi-100m-leads`** says: the **Rule of 100** — 100 outreach actions
  every day for 100 days; "disciplined execution over creativity". Context:
  founder-operator, SMB/consumer/agency/local services, lower ACV, no SDR team.
- **These are mathematically incompatible.** 100 emails × 10 minutes = 16+
  hours per day.
- **Resolution rule:** **Price point and who is sending decide.** High ACV,
  small named list, professional seller → Lavender: research every email,
  accept low volume. Low ACV, large addressable market, founder doing their own
  outreach → Hormozi: volume with light personalization, accept lower reply
  rates per send. Read `context/business.md` §1 (price point) and §6 (who does
  the selling) before choosing. **Never blend them** — a half-researched email
  at half volume loses both advantages: it is neither relevant enough to reply
  to nor voluminous enough to work statistically.

## Low-pressure ask vs. clear CTA

- **`josh-braun`** says: close soft. "No rush, but is this worth a conversation
  in the next quarter?" / "might you be open to talking about this?" The
  mechanism is avoiding re-triggering resistance at the last line.
- **`lavender-benchmarks`** says: use "clear, low-friction calls-to-action" and
  names vague CTAs like "learn more" as a failure mode for technical buyers.
- **Resolution rule:** These conflict less than they appear — Lavender's target
  is *vagueness about what you're asking for*, Braun's is *pressure*. The
  resolution is **specific about the ask, soft about the timing**. "No rush,
  but is this worth a conversation in the next quarter?" is specific (a
  conversation) and unpressured (no rush). "Learn more" fails because it names
  no action. Avoid "Let's find 15 minutes" — Lavender flags it as a cliché and
  it is also the pressure Braun warns about.

## Social proof — logos or not

- **`gong-cold-email-data`** says: include "relevant, specific logos or
  industries similar to the prospect's" in the body; social proof
  **underperforms in subject lines**.
- **`lavender-benchmarks`** says: proof lands differently by department —
  case studies work for Operations, Marketing, Research, Product, Legal;
  **Finance is logo-skeptical** and dislikes name-dropping. Use the **BAR**
  method (Background, Actions, Results) rather than listing names.
- **Resolution rule:** No contradiction on placement (both: body, not subject).
  Refine by recipient: default to a specific similar logo per Gong, but for
  Finance, and any audience Lavender marks logo-skeptical, convert it to a BAR
  narrative with a number instead of a name.

## Layer note — Hormozi is not competing with the others

`hormozi-100m-leads` operates one layer up from the rest of this corpus. It
decides **which channel** you use (Core Four) and **at what volume** (Rule of
100). Gong, Lavender, and Josh Braun decide **what the message says** once
you've chosen cold outreach. Only the personalization-vs-volume conflict above
is a genuine collision; elsewhere they stack rather than compete.
