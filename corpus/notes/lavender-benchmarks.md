# Lavender — cold email benchmarks (231,818 emails / ~50,000 inboxes)

## 1. Core thesis
Cold email performance is driven by mechanical, measurable properties — length,
subject-line construction, mobile formatting, reading effort — and by matching
the message to the recipient's **seniority and department**, which change what
"good" means. There is no universal best practice: "Align the value to the
altitude." Most emails fail before they are read, on formatting and phrasing
that trip the reader's mental spam filter.

## 2. Operating frameworks

### Align value to the altitude
- **Use when:** Choosing what to say to any given recipient.
- **Steps:**
  1. **C-Suite / VPs** → company strategy, revenue, cost, risk.
  2. **Senior leaders** → department-level use cases.
  3. **Managers** → tactical application, not strategy.
  4. **Individual contributors** → impact on their own KPIs.
- **Failure mode:** One message reused across altitudes.

### The 5x5x5 personalization process
- **Use when:** Researching before writing.
- **Steps:**
  1. **Research, 5 minutes** — gather facts from multiple sources.
  2. **Document** — record both the fact and its source, as you go.
  3. **Write, 5 minutes** — only after research is finished.
- **Source prioritization:** rank sources as **Go-to's** (reliable AND
  relevant) → **Reliables** (consistently valuable) → **Gems** (unique but
  occasional).
- **Why it works (per source):** Finishing research before writing "prevents
  temptation to write prematurely."
- **Payoff:** personalizing vs. sending a template = **50%–250% increase in
  reply rates**.
- **Failure mode:** Over-personalizing — including every detail found, being
  "too on the nose", leading with irrelevant personal details.

### Good personalization = observation → challenge
- **Structure:** You noticed X, therefore you suspect they face challenge Y.
  The observation must logically connect to the rest of the message.

### The BAR method (proof)
- **Background, Actions, Results** — contextualize proof rather than listing
  client names.
- **Use when:** Inserting social proof, especially for Operations, Marketing,
  Research, Product, Legal (strong case-study responders). Finance is
  logo-skeptical.

## 3. Verbatim language patterns

| Phrase / pattern | Where it goes | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| "Template Revisions" / "Reply Rate Question" / "Personalization Issue" / "Rep Writing" / "Ramp" / "Email Data Thoughts" | subject line — **GOOD** | 1-3 words, title case, neutral, reads like an internal email |
| "Will, have 15 minutes?" / "Struggling to get replies?" / "2x Reply Rates" / "Wish you could Personalize faster?" / "Accelerate Pipeline Coverage" | subject line — **ANTI-PATTERN** | First name, question, number, command, marketing language |
| "I saw your product includes live support. As a young product-led org, I wondered if..." | opener | Observation → challenge, without forced familiarity |
| "Are you wrapping all releases in flags today or just risky ones?" | body — to engineers | Specific workflow question; concrete, not jargon |
| "With the new freemium tier, I imagine the team's expecting a lot of new product data." | body — to product leaders | Ties to roadmap priority before any technical detail |
| "Read the post on moving to weekly releases. Big shift." | opener — to executives | Direct, credible, no warmth padding |
| "I hope this finds you well" | opener — **ANTI-PATTERN** | Cliché; signals a pitch |
| "Let's find 15 minutes" | close — **ANTI-PATTERN** | Cliché; signals a pitch |
| "strategic revenue enablement", "rigor", "world-class", "cutting-edge" | body — **ANTI-PATTERN** | Buzzwords that obscure value in the <6 seconds you get |
| "learn more" | CTA — **ANTI-PATTERN** | Vague CTA; named as a failure for technical buyers |

## 4. Objection handling
Source gives none — first-touch email only.

## 5. Non-negotiables and prohibitions
- **DO NOT** put a question in the subject line — lowers open rate by **56%**.
- **DO NOT** put numbers in the subject line — reduces opens by **46.33%**.
- **DO NOT** use punctuation (? !) in the subject line — reduces opens by **36%**.
- **DO NOT** use the recipient's first name in the subject line — **12% fewer replies**.
- **DO NOT** drop title case in the subject line — reduces opens by **30%**.
- **DO NOT** use emojis, commands, superlatives, or clichés in subject lines.
- **DO NOT** open with "I hope this finds you well" or a name introduction.
- **DO NOT** close with "Let's find 15 minutes."
- **DO NOT** use buzzwords or jargon — including with engineers; technical
  jargon "turns engineers away."
- **DO NOT** adopt an educating/informative tone — "informative tones have a
  perfectly inverse correlation with your reply rate."
- **DO NOT** send large paragraphs — readers open on phones **8x** more often
  than on computers.
- **DO NOT** over-excite researchers or hype finance professionals.
- **DO NOT** use vague CTAs like "learn more."

## 7. Metrics & benchmarks
**Dataset:** 231,818 cold emails, ~50,000 active inboxes, as of 2026-02-04,
excluding auto-replies.

**Length & attention**
- Optimal cold email length: **25–50 words**.
- Readers spend **11 seconds** on an email; you have **<6 seconds** to convey value.
- Follow-ups with **4+ sentences get 15x more meetings** than follow-ups with 3 or fewer.
- Mobile-optimized emails get **83% more replies** on average.
- Emails opened on phones **8x** more often than on computers.

**Subject lines**
- Optimal: **1–3 words**, 2 optimal. Two words → four words = **-17.5% replies**.
- Penalties: questions **-56% opens**; numbers **-46.33% opens**;
  punctuation **-36% opens**; no title case **-30% opens**; first name **-12% replies**.

**Personalization**
- Personalizing vs. templating: **+50% to +250% reply rate**.

**By department / seniority**
- Operations leaders: **5.4%** reply on A-grade emails (**+58% lift**).
- Finance: **+79% lift** (highest of all personas), but only **6.1%** of finance
  emails earn an "A".
- Technical buyers overall: **5.2%** baseline → **5.5%** on A-grade (+6%).
- Technical ICs: **5.3%** → **8.0%** (+49%).
- Technical managers: **4.3%** → **6.3%** (+49%).
- Technical directors: **3.4%** → **8.4%** (highest absolute lift).
- HR: **3.4%** reply rate — "one of the hardest departments to cold email."

**Mobile formatting impact** — high for Sales, Product, Marketing, Operations,
frontline managers; low for Engineering, Research, Finance, Legal, HR,
Facilities, PR.

**Tone by department** — HR: warm, avoid transactional. Sales: concise, direct.
Legal/Finance: precise, matter-of-fact. Researchers: measured, restrained.

**Proof by department** — case studies land with Operations, Marketing,
Research, Product, Legal. Finance dislikes name-dropping. Sales, Engineering,
Facilities want use cases.

## 8. Context limits
B2B, drawn from Lavender's own users (sellers already using an email coaching
tool — a self-selected, above-average population). Departmental findings assume
a company large enough to have distinct functions. Everything here is
first-touch and follow-up **email**; nothing on calls, discovery, or closing.
Lavender's own caveat: "Lavender's analysis of your inbox is the only true
benchmarking" — results vary with offer quality.
